# Subcult Research Home

## The recommendation

Build **Subcult**, shared infrastructure for independent culture: a participant-facing discovery experience and a private **Studio** for recurring cultural teams. Use Subcults for public identities, scenes, touring and AT publishing; bring Subcult OS's production workflows into the same product through explicit integration contracts.

The central hypothesis is continuity: the same team can invite a community, coordinate an event, preserve its history and organize the next one with less repeated work. Openness supports the product; it is not a substitute for customer value.

## What materially changed in the expanded research

OpenMeet already publishes public events through community event lexicons. Laylo is not the only audience competitor: Hive, Openstage and Bandsintown put additional pressure on a generic CRM offer. Professional operations are also an established category. These findings narrow the plausible advantage to segment fit, workflow continuity, privacy and credible interoperability.

Trade-association research points to financially constrained venues, making support cost and willingness to pay first-order concerns. This strengthens the case for a small regional pilot, coexistence with ticketing providers, and explicit stop criteria before adding expensive modules.

Use the proposal as a connected set of testable decisions. Do not convert every attractive idea into a launch requirement.
