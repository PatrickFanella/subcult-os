# Subcult General Ticketing Research

## Eventbrite

Eventbrite is a baseline for public event discovery, organizer tooling and commerce. Its US pricing page was observed during the initial proposal research with 3.7% + $1.79 per paid ticket and 2.9% processing per order; the later refresh returned HTTP 429. Treat those figures as an earlier observed snapshot, not a freshly confirmed quote. Different fee bases mean they should not be collapsed into one simplistic rate. [Official pricing](https://www.eventbrite.com/organizer/pricing/), [features](https://www.eventbrite.com/organizer/features/all-features/).

The important comparison is net organizer payout, total attendee price, discovery value and support at the actual event configuration.

## Humanitix

The US page lists a standard platform component of 2.1% + $0.99 per paid ticket, with payment processing displayed separately at 2.9% + $0.30. It also lists free events at no charge and a distinct charity/school tier. Do not assume all percentages and fixed charges apply to identical bases or every geography. [Humanitix US pricing](https://humanitix.com/us/pricing).

**Implication:** cultural alignment alone is not unique. A mission-led ticketing proposition already exists, alongside broad organizer features.

## Ticket Tailor

Ticket Tailor describes prepaid/pay-as-you-sell ticket usage pricing rather than a mandatory monthly subscription, and distinguishes its fees from payment-provider processing. A precise comparison needs currency, volume and payment setup. [Pricing explanation](https://help.tickettailor.com/en/articles/6731528-how-much-does-ticket-tailor-cost-and-how-will-i-be-charged), [subscription explanation](https://help.tickettailor.com/en/articles/1550144-do-you-have-a-yearly-subscription).

**Implication:** small or irregular organizers can reasonably prefer variable expense over another monthly bill. A subscription must earn its keep between events.

## Comparison worksheet

For a target event, record ticket count, price tiers, free/comp allocation, average tickets per order, tax, discounts, refunds and chargebacks. Then obtain actual all-in quotes under the intended jurisdiction and contract. Keep buyer-paid and organizer-paid fees separate.

Do not rank providers with a spreadsheet that treats “per ticket” and “per order” charges as interchangeable. Include the cost of customer support and payout timing, which may matter more than a small nominal fee difference.

## Subcult decision

Do not build native ticketing simply to advertise a lower fee. Native commerce adds fraud, disputes, reconciliation, consumer support and night-of risk. Start with the linked operations value and expand when a specific unmet need justifies the burden.
