# Subcult Eventeny Research

## Established category overlap

Eventeny presents an integrated event-management platform spanning applications, vendors, volunteers, mapping and ticketing. This is direct evidence against a broad novelty claim that nobody combines event coordination with commerce. [Eventeny overview](https://www.eventeny.com/why-eventeny/).

Its fee explanation distinguishes platform fees across transaction types and processing fees. Those distinctions should be preserved when making a real quote; vendor payments, ticketing and other transactions are not interchangeable. [Eventeny fee documentation](https://help.eventeny.com/en/articles/14743975-eventeny-s-fees-explained).

## Relevance to Subcult

Community festivals and cultural markets may value applications and role management as much as artist discovery. They can also bring much larger complexity: stall placement, vendor documentation, capacity and dependencies across many workers.

Subcult OS's applications and staffing are useful foundations, but that does not establish parity with a mature festival workflow. Avoid using one matching screen name as evidence of equivalent capability.

## Entry boundary

The initial proposal should serve smaller recurring cultural events before attempting comprehensive festival management. A small festival could join the pilot only if its required workflows fit safely within the supported scope and there is a fallback for critical operations.

A future festival module should be justified by repeated demand across several paying teams, not one bespoke customer. Explicitly estimate maintenance cost for forms, approvals, attachments, role permissions and change notification.

## Competitive questions

Can an organizer reuse people and plans between events? How do invited collaborators see only their assignments? What audit history survives an application change? What information can be exported? Which map or check-in behavior works under poor connectivity?

These are questions for demonstrations, not assertions of missing Eventeny functionality.

## Product lesson

“All-in-one” sells breadth but creates a responsibility to make every included step dependable. Subcult should offer a clear core with optional modules and honest capability boundaries. An integration with an established vendor-management product may be a better decision than rebuilding it.
