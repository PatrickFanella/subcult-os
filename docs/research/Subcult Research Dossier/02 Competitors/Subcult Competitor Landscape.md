## The realistic comparison

A buyer may compare Subcult not against one vendor but against a bundle: an event seller, a chat group, a campaign tool and spreadsheets. Replacing every component simultaneously raises switching risk. A credible entry point can sit between them and reduce one repeated burden first.

Open distribution is an architectural difference, but its customer benefit must be concrete: an event can appear in another compatible app, a public identity can remain usable outside our service, or another calendar can interpret a correction. “Decentralized” alone does not tell an operator what improves tonight.

## Competitor demos to run next

Use the same scenario: a recurring night with two co-hosts, a touring artist, three crew roles, a protected address, an opted-in announcement list, a reschedule and a financial closeout.

Ask each relevant supplier to demonstrate its part. Record which steps require an additional product, elevated role, paid plan or manual export. Do not infer feature absence from a marketing page that simply does not discuss it.

## Potential advantage versus table stakes

Potential advantage: coherent continuity for independent teams, explicit private/public boundaries, useful public interoperability and low-friction coexistence.

Table stakes: mobile usability, accurate dates/timezones, opt-out handling, accessible registration, clear prices, reliable payments and recoverable data. Those are not optional merely because the product is culturally distinctive.
