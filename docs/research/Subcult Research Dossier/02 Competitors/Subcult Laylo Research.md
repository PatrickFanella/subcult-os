# Subcult Laylo Research

## What is established

Laylo markets branded Drop pages, artist/fan signups and notifications around releases, tours and other moments. Its positioning directly overlaps Subcult's proposed Signal and audience workflow. [Laylo Drops](https://laylo.com/features/drops).

Laylo's own comparison page advertises Pro at $25/month plus messaging, with unlimited contacts, CRM and integrations. Treat that as a vendor-advertised starting point: the dedicated pricing page did not expose readable full terms in this research, and no checkout was completed. [Laylo comparison](https://laylo.com/compare/cobrand), [pricing entry](https://laylo.com/pricing).

Its Hive comparison page advertises import/export and ticket/commerce integrations. This is evidence of Laylo's own claims, not a neutral source for Hive's limitations. [Laylo event-marketing comparison](https://laylo.com/compare/hive).

## Why it matters

Laylo is a direct competitor if Subcult sells “create a campaign page and text your fans.” An artist already using it may see another contact-capture tool as redundant. Product breadth would not automatically offset its focused campaign experience, partner relationships or user familiarity.

The lesson is to make the announcement journey extremely clear: what a person signed up for, which contact point is verified, when a message will arrive, and how to stop it. A cluttered “community operating system” can lose before broader benefits become visible.

## Proposed response

Do not start by recreating every presave, Instagram and SMS automation. Start with a complete email or web-push journey linked to an actual occurrence and the team's work. Let existing Laylo users keep their campaign tooling during an operations pilot.

Differentiate on context and continuity: an artist's appearance in a local scene, chosen city interests, linked crew commitments, and an approved public archive. The value must remain understandable without a protocol explanation.

Do not claim that Laylo traps all audience data or lacks export. Do not quote vendor conversion multipliers as expected Subcult growth. Do not presume a documented integration means we can obtain the same API access.

## Open demo questions

1. What gets exported: contact details, consent wording, sender scope, timestamps, suppression and delivery history?
2. Can two artist/venue teams collaborate without implicitly sharing marketing permission?
3. How are changed/cancelled event messages separated from promotions?
4. Which integration permissions and commercial contracts are required?
5. What is the full recurring cost at a realistic audience size and message cadence?
6. What can a touring artist reuse city-to-city without exposing private local-team information?

## Build, integrate or avoid

Build the small relationship/announcement flow required for the pilot. Investigate integration only after a partner asks for it and authorized access is available. Avoid a generic “better Laylo” claim: Subcult would need demonstrated acquisition, deliverability and usability superiority to sustain it.
