# Subcult Go to Market

## Start with one connected circuit

Recruit five to ten recurring teams in a region where artists, venues and crew already interact. Access to willing operators matters more than nominal city size. The region remains an open decision; no outreach has been sent.

Seek variation within a coherent market: a small venue, a recurring collective, a promoter and an artist team. Avoid averaging incompatible needs into a giant feature list.

## Initial offer

Offer a scoped pilot around the team's next two events: document its current process, configure the minimal workflow, observe use and compare the closeout. Keep ticketing unchanged unless an authorized integration is essential and safe.

Do not offer unlimited concierge labor or custom features as hidden conditions of adoption. Record support time and distinguish product value from a founder personally organizing the event.

## Acquisition hypothesis

Begin through existing organizer relationships and explicit referrals, not broad paid acquisition. A successful event can introduce another host or artist, but that referral loop is a hypothesis to measure.

Public event records may enable discovery in compatible apps. Do not model free protocol distribution as guaranteed traffic, sales or a viral growth coefficient.

Potential later channels include cultural associations, venue networks, artist managers and integration partners. None has agreed to distribute Subcult. Partner discussions require separate authorization.

## Onboarding sequence

1. Identify the operator and budget holder.
2. Capture the existing workflow without importing unnecessary personal data.
3. Choose one event and a safe fallback.
4. Create the public/private mapping and invite minimal collaborators.
5. Test one announcement with synthetic recipients before real delivery.
6. Run the event and close it out.
7. Reuse the plan at the second event.
8. Ask for payment/renewal under an explicit scope.

## Positioning language to test

“Keep your community connected and your next event organized.”

“Your artists, your scenes, your production plan—without replacing your ticket seller.”

Avoid promises such as “own every fan,” “never miss a message,” “fully decentralized business operations” or “guaranteed more ticket sales.”

## Expansion rule

Add a module only when several teams repeatedly encounter the same unmet need and are willing to fund its ongoing cost. Expansion can be deeper reliability, not more categories.

A healthy early result may be a smaller operations product plus public publishing. Be willing to reduce breadth if that is what customers actually value.
