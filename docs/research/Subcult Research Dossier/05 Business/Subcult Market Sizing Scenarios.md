# Subcult Market Sizing Scenarios

## What this model does and does not size

The relevant unit is a **paying organizational workspace**, not an attendee, artist profile, event listing or dollar of ticket sales. The initial geography is a to-be-selected regional circuit; the intended buyer is a recurring independent cultural team.

No verified census of eligible, reachable and willing-to-pay workspaces was obtained. Consequently this dossier does **not** claim a measured TAM, SAM or SOM. Industry economic-impact reports are useful context, but they cannot be relabeled as addressable subscription revenue.

The model below is a transparent planning sensitivity, not a forecast or confidence interval.

## Bottom-up model

Annual subscription opportunity = eligible distinct paying workspaces × paid adoption × annual revenue per paying workspace.

For a real SAM, first deduplicate venue/collective/promoter/artist entities, identify their actual purchasing units, exclude incompatible workflows/contracts, and establish reachable geography. For an attainable launch cohort, constrain the result by onboarding capacity and sales access.

## Illustrative sensitivity

Every input in this table is a hypothetical assumption. The 1,000-workspace universe is a round modeling unit, not a measured market count.

| Hypothetical eligible workspaces | Paid adoption | Paying workspaces | Monthly ARPA | Annualized subscriptions |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 | 2% | 20 | $39 | $9,360 |
| 1,000 | 5% | 50 | $59 | $35,400 |
| 1,000 | 10% | 100 | $99 | $118,800 |

Example: 1,000 × 0.05 × $59 × 12 = $35,400. The endpoints vary both adoption and ARPA; they are scenarios, not an isolated one-variable sensitivity.

At a fixed 50 paying workspaces, $39/$59/$99 monthly ARPA yields $23,400/$35,400/$59,400 annualized subscriptions. At fixed $59 ARPA, 20/50/100 paying workspaces yields $14,160/$35,400/$70,800. This shows separately how price and adoption affect the result.

## Capacity cross-check

If a team can onboard only four new workspaces per month, even perfect demand caps gross additions at 48 per year before churn. This is an illustrative capacity assumption. Founder-led interviews and hand-holding must not be modeled as infinitely scalable.

Multiple public scenes may belong to one paying organization. Conversely, a venue and visiting promoter may pay separately if they retain separate workspaces. Deduplicate based on buying behavior, not public graph nodes.

## Evidence priorities

Build an authorized prospect sample in one territory; record event cadence, decision maker, existing tools, budget range, contract restrictions and onboarding burden. Do not scrape or populate private contact lists into this public dossier.

Interview non-buyers as well as enthusiasts. Replace one assumption at a time with evidence. If the reachable segment is too small or price-sensitive, narrow the service cost or change the segment before inflating a national denominator.
