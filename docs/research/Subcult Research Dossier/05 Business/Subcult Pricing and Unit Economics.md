# Subcult Pricing and Unit Economics

All Subcult prices, cost inputs and customer counts below are **hypotheses**. They are not existing tariffs, measured margins or forecasts.

## Proposed packaging

| Package | Test price | Intended value |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Participant | Free | Discovery, event access, chosen relationships |
| Collective | $39/workspace/month | Recurring production plan, commitments and basic audience workflow |
| Venue | $99/workspace/month | Higher recurring operational use, team controls and reporting |
| Network | $249+/month, later | Multi-workspace coordination only after demonstrated demand |

Avoid charging participants merely to preserve a portable public identity. Treat paid messaging and premium services separately. Do not promise unlimited SMS inside a low-price plan.

Use competitor prices as context, not cost-based proof of a viable business. See [[Subcult Laylo Research]], [[Subcult Audience Platform Research]] and [[Subcult Venue Software Research]].

## Subscription scenarios

| Assumed customers | MRR | Annualized recurring revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 50 Collective + 20 Venue | $3,930 | $47,160 |
| 250 Collective + 100 Venue | $19,650 | $235,800 |

Formulas: MRR = Collective count × 39 + Venue count × 99. Annualized revenue = MRR × 12. These exclude churn, discounts, taxes, trials, delivery revenue, transaction fees and non-recurring services. They do not imply those customer counts are obtainable.

## Contribution margin sensitivity

Illustrative Venue workspace: $99 subscription less $8 allocated variable infrastructure/provider cost less support labor at $40/hour.

| Monthly support time | Contribution before fixed costs | Contribution rate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.5 hours | $71 | 71.7% |
| 1 hour | $51 | 51.5% |
| 2 hours | $11 | 11.1% |

This is not measured COGS or accounting gross margin. It omits fixed engineering, sales, insurance and other overhead. It demonstrates why night-of support can overwhelm a small subscription.

At the Collective price, the same $8 cost and 0.5 hours of support leave $11 before fixed costs. A cheap tier needs a genuinely low-touch workflow.

## Messaging

Estimate cost from recipients × sends × message segments × destination-specific rate, plus carrier/registration/number costs where applicable. Track provider acceptance, delivery and retries separately.

Example only: 2,000 recipients × 2 sends × 1 segment × $0.02 = $80 before extra charges. The rate is a scenario input, not a selected-provider quote. Longer or Unicode messages can affect segmentation under provider rules; confirm the exact implementation before billing customers.

Keep variable costs transparent. For early pilots, email and web push may reduce cash expense, but still require delivery, consent and support work.

## Commercial tests

Ask for a real paid commitment at a named package with a defined scope. Record who approved it and what existing expense or operational burden it replaces. A discounted design-partner service arrangement is not evidence of scalable SaaS pricing.

Avoid forecasting lifetime value from a few weeks of pilot retention. Track acquisition hours and onboarding cost directly until cohorts mature.
