# Subcult Customer and Market Thesis

## First segment

Target recurring independent cultural teams with a named operator, a shared calendar and repeated coordination work. A useful initial profile is a collective or small venue running events often enough to reuse plans, but without a dedicated enterprise operations team.

This segment definition is a hypothesis. Do not use venue capacity alone to infer purchasing power. A volunteer-led team with a small room can have complex workflows and little budget; a larger operator may be constrained by existing contracts.

## Users are not always buyers

| Role | Job | Likely concern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Venue/collective lead | Keep events viable and the team aligned | Cost, migration effort, night-of reliability |
| Producer/promoter | Confirm people, timing and commitments | Re-entry, missing ownership, last-minute changes |
| Artist/manager | Maintain relationships across cities | Correct credits, scope of access, contact permission |
| Door/crew | Know what to do now | Fast mobile access, poor connectivity, minimal permissions |
| Participant | Find a relevant event and attend confidently | Clear price, location, safety, unwanted messages |

An attendee's enthusiasm does not validate the budget holder. A promoter's wish list does not prove a venue can replace its ticket contract.

## Economic context

NIVA's 2025 State of Live outreach material reports that 64% of independent stages were not profitable in 2024. This concerns the study's independent-live-sector scope, not all event businesses and not Subcult's addressable software market. [NIVA research toolkit](https://www.nivassoc.org/stateoflive/findings-toolkit).

Music Venue Trust's 2025 annual-report announcement describes average UK grassroots venue margins of 2.5%. The UK geography and grassroots definition matter; this is context for price sensitivity, not a US revenue assumption. [MVT report announcement](https://www.musicvenuetrust.com/2026/01/music-venue-trust-annual-report-2025/?sfid=htst).

**Inference:** time savings alone may not create budget. A purchase must connect to a tangible operational improvement, existing expense or revenue-protection need. Expensive concierge onboarding can easily consume the margin from a low monthly fee.

## Beachhead selection

Prefer one regional circuit where a small number of organizers share artists and crew, while retaining separate finances. The original Chicago/Milwaukee/Detroit example is illustrative, not a validated launch territory.

Choose a location based on access to willing partners, event frequency, ticketing flexibility and operator availability. Do not choose based solely on population or social follower counts.

Exclude from the first pilot: major festivals with high consequence multi-gate scanning, exclusive-ticketing replacements, large multi-entity accounting, public/private identity migrations for real customers, and events requiring unsupported safety-critical delivery.

## Research questions

Ask what went wrong at the last event, what evidence survives, who fixed it, how much work repeated, and who could approve a purchase. Compare high-frequency paid teams with low-frequency volunteer teams; averaging their answers can conceal incompatible businesses.
