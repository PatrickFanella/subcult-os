# Subcult Executive Proposal

## Proposition

Subcult connects the people, places and work that keep independent culture alive. It combines public discovery and creator-controlled cultural records with private tools for the recurring work of putting on events.

The product has two coherent faces: a free participant experience for finding scenes, artists and gatherings, and a paid Studio for teams coordinating audiences and production. The same event links the two without exposing private contacts, staffing, precise protected locations or financial information.

This is broader than an event listing app but narrower than a general social network. The first market is independent music and adjacent cultural communities: recurring promoters, collectives, small venues, artist teams and community arts spaces.

## Why this could matter

An event is not an isolated transaction. Before it, people form relationships and make commitments. During it, a team must deliver. After it, knowledge, credits and financial obligations need continuity. Our hypothesis is that teams lose time and trust when these stages are split across loosely connected tools.

The proposed loop is:

Discovery → chosen connection → relevant announcement → participation → coordinated production → useful archive → next gathering.

The public graph can connect artists, appearances, scenes and places across applications. Private operations remain accountable to the people doing the work. A participant can attend without becoming a public profile, a marketing lead for every partner, or an unpaid advocate for the protocol.

## Existing foundation

Subcults contains a richer public music domain, audience/consent primitives and AT publishing/synchronization work. Subcult OS contains workspaces, commitments, staffing, reservations, check-in, settlement and archives. These are source-observed assets, not a completed integrated product. [[Subcult Repository Evidence]] records the exact boundary.

Reuse the two systems through one identity-linking policy and a stable occurrence-to-operations mapping. Do not begin with a repository merge. Distinguish public event state, private operational state, publication status and ticket validity.

## Competitive thesis

Laylo establishes the standard for artist audience activation. Hive and Openstage deepen that competition. Luma and Partiful establish expectations for quick event creation. RA and DICE compete for music discovery and ticketing relationships. Opendate and Prism already address professional operations. OpenMeet and Smoke Signal demonstrate open event publishing.

Accordingly, “all-in-one,” “own your audience,” and “events on AT” are insufficient differentiators. The claim to test is: **Subcult gives recurring independent cultural teams a simpler continuity layer across their public community and private production work, without forcing them to replace ticketing or surrender public identity.** See [[Subcult Competitor Landscape]].

## Initial offer

Start with one joined journey: publish a public occurrence, open its production record, assign commitments, send one requested announcement, use an existing ticket provider, complete a private closeout, and publish approved credits. Carry a reusable template into the next event.

Public participation remains free. Test workspace subscriptions around $39 for collectives and $99 for venues, with a later network tier only if demand warrants it. These are research hypotheses, not market-validated prices. Meter expensive messaging separately; avoid relying on speculative ticket commissions.

## Pilot and investment gates

Recruit five to ten design partners in one connected regional music circuit. Observe real recent workflows before pitching. Advance only when at least five teams complete two event cycles, at least three make a genuine paid commitment, and safety and support economics hold. These are proposed decision thresholds, not statistically conclusive proof.

Prioritize calendar interoperability and sync fixtures as public contributions. Treat permissioned Spaces, organization delegation and portable consent as bounded research, not dependencies for launch.

The immediate request is approval of a limited discovery-and-integration phase, not authorization for a full rewrite, public launch, commercial outreach or upstream submissions.
