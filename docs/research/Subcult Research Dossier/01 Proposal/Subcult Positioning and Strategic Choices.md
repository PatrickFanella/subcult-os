# Subcult Positioning and Strategic Choices

## Positioning statement

For recurring independent cultural teams who coordinate people as well as sell attendance, Subcult is a community-and-production workspace that preserves continuity from public discovery to the next event. Unlike a standalone campaign page or ticket checkout, the proposed product connects cultural context with explicit commitments and reusable closeout. Unlike a broad professional venue suite, it aims to start with the smallest recurring team and expand only when needed.

This is a proposed position, not an established competitive advantage. Its validity depends on interviews and repeat use.

## Strategic choices

**Broad product, narrow customer.** Add capabilities around the same team's recurring work. Do not chase conferences, weddings, municipal procurement and arena ticketing simultaneously.

**Coexistence before replacement.** A host may need its current ticket seller for contracts, discovery or familiarity. Start with authorized links/imports, show data freshness, and leave provider-controlled actions with the provider. A competitor can also be an integration partner.

**Open public records, private accountability.** Make public identity and cultural records interpretable outside Subcult. Keep finances and team authorization explicit. Public authorship is not a shortcut around participant privacy.

**Useful without network effects.** A single collective should get value from commitments and a reusable event plan even when no other scene uses Subcult. Public discovery should improve with participation, not be a prerequisite for basic utility.

**A human-scaled relationship model.** Prefer declared interests, transparent recommendations and chosen cities over inferred personal surveillance. Do not create a universal reliability score from private work history.

## Defensibility

Code and lexicons can be copied. Potential defensibility comes from trusted organizer relationships, workflow fit, dependable integration/recovery, and local density of useful public records. That is an execution thesis; none is secured merely by choosing AT Protocol.

Openness intentionally reduces lock-in. Therefore retention must come from service quality and cumulative organizational usefulness. Export should remain useful enough that leaving is real, not a marketing claim.

## What would falsify this position

If teams use only announcement pages, a focused audience tool may be the correct product. If operators do not want audience responsibilities in the same system, offer modules without forcing one buyer. If the important money is in complex deal-making, the target may need Prism/Opendate rather than a new broad platform.

If the only interested users are AT developers, revisit customer selection before expanding infrastructure. Technical enthusiasm is not evidence of independent venues' willingness to pay.
