# Subcult Customer Interview Plan

## Purpose and sample

Test whether recurring independent cultural teams have a costly continuity problem and whether the proposed buyer will pay to solve it. Start with roughly twelve interviews across venue leads, collectives/promoters, artist teams and crew. This is a qualitative learning sample, not representative market research.

Include people satisfied with existing tools and people who decline the pilot. Record segment and event frequency so conflicting needs are not averaged away.

## Forty-five minute structure

**Context, five minutes:** role, event cadence, team structure and who pays for tools.

**Last event, fifteen minutes:** walk through the most recent completed event from initial idea to closeout. Ask to see non-sensitive artifacts where permission allows. Capture handoffs, repeated data entry, missing owners and errors.

**Existing tools, ten minutes:** what each tool does, what it costs, what is contractual and what cannot move. Ask about the last attempted tool change and why it succeeded or failed.

**Concept, ten minutes:** show the short product story only after collecting the baseline. Ask which part solves a real problem, which is unnecessary and what would stop adoption.

**Commitment, five minutes:** ask whether a scoped two-event pilot is useful, who must approve it and what they would pay under a clear offer. Do not pressure participants into consent or infer a purchase from politeness.

## Questions worth asking

“What did you have to confirm more than once?”

“What information was wrong on the day, and who fixed it?”

“What was still unresolved a week later?”

“Which subscription would you keep if you could only keep one?”

“What would need to be true before you trusted this at the door?”

“Would you use the production workflow if no new audience discovered you through it?”

Avoid “Would you like an all-in-one app?” It invites agreement without revealing tradeoffs.

## Privacy and analysis

Ask permission before recording. Store identifying information outside the shareable dossier. Use pseudonymous interview IDs, separate observed facts from interpretation, and retain contrary evidence.

Synthesize by job and buyer segment, not by counting feature votes. A painful anecdote needs frequency and consequence before it becomes a roadmap priority.
