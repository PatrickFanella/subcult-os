# Subcult Product Journeys

These journeys are proposed acceptance scenarios, not demonstrations of a finished unified product.

## Participant: discover without surrendering privacy

A participant chooses Detroit and a date range. They find a touring artist's local appearance through a scene or venue. The artist's home territory does not determine where this show appears.

The person sees local time, access information, the organizer, status and a clear ticket destination. They can attend as a guest. Saving the show, publicly RSVPing, joining a scene and requesting future messages are separate actions. A protected address is not exposed on a public map.

Success means an unfamiliar participant can answer where, when, who and how to attend without understanding AT Protocol or receiving unexpected marketing. Failure includes a stale cancelled event, timezone ambiguity or accidental publication of attendance.

## Organizer: one event, connected work

A host publishes the occurrence and opens its linked private operations record. The public page can credit several hosts; the operations record has one owning workspace and explicitly invited collaborators.

The host assigns sound, door and artist confirmation, with an owner and due time for each commitment. Collaborators see the minimum information needed for their work. No public co-host credit creates private access.

Success means fewer missing owners and repeated confirmations than the team's prior workflow. Count actual handoffs and corrections, not completed form fields.

## Artist: continuity across a tour

An artist's Act appears in several locally hosted events. Each appearance retains its own city, time, status and host. The artist can request corrections without overwriting a venue's financial record.

A Detroit-only announcement subscription does not become permission for unrelated national promotions. Tour interest is declared interest, not verified attendance or a ticket sale.

Success means reusable identity and clear local ownership. Failure includes duplicated occurrences, incorrect local discovery or one team's control leaking into another's workspace.

## Crew: useful under pressure

A door worker opens a restricted mobile view. They can see their assignment and supported entry workflow, not the mailing list or settlement. If connectivity fails, the interface states what is cached and what cannot be verified.

If native offline validation is unqualified, the event uses its ticket provider's supported door tooling. A manual fallback must be rehearsed before live use.

## Closeout: private history, approved public credits

The host reconciles actuals and unresolved obligations, then retains a private report. A separate publishing step selects credits, images and links for the public archive, with approval from relevant contributors.

The next event can reuse its plan without copying outdated consent or inherited access permissions. This is the continuity benefit to measure over two cycles.
