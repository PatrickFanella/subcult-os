# Subcult MVP Scope

## The smallest complete product

A recurring team should be able to publish one event, coordinate its commitments, contact people who requested a relevant announcement, complete a private closeout and reuse the plan next time.

This is not the minimum number of screens. It is the minimum joined workflow that tests the business thesis.

## Include

- Public event page and list/map discovery with correct place, time, status and source.
- Existing-account or ordinary guest participation without protocol education.
- Authenticated event-to-workspace mapping with explicit owner and collaborators.
- Commitments and a small crew assignment workflow.
- One verified contact channel and a complete announcement/withdrawal journey.
- Link to the existing ticket provider; authorized minimal import only where available.
- Private closeout plus separately approved public credits.
- Data export, access revocation, recovery and basic operational monitoring.
- One independent public-record reader for the chosen interoperability scope.

## Exclude initially

Native ticket-marketplace replacement, complex festival vendor management, dynamic pricing, resale, multi-entity accounting, generalized reputation, opaque fan scoring, paid memberships, a proprietary social feed, live audio as a core dependency, and production Spaces storage.

Do not eliminate a safety requirement merely to fit this scope. Instead remove the feature that would require it.

## Acceptance gate

Five proposed design-partner teams each complete two event cycles with an honest fallback plan. At least three make a paid commitment without custom development promises. Record time saved, repeat usage, unresolved duties, support burden and unwanted-contact incidents.

These thresholds are pragmatic early gates, not a statistically representative validation. A privacy or financial defect can stop progression even if engagement metrics look strong.

## Sequencing principle

Begin with the team workflow that yields value without a large public audience. Add discovery distribution and communication around real events, not empty city maps. A standalone public page should still be useful when Studio is not used.

Do not make every participant create an account to help measure engagement. Measure successfully completed jobs and voluntary return rather than forced registrations.
