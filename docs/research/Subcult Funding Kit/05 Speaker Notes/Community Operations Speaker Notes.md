# Community Operations Speaker Notes
Companion to [[Community Operations Deck]]. Target length: 5–7 minutes. State that this is a proposal and distinguish prototypes from qualified services.

## Slide 1: Community Operations
Open with the beneficiary, not the protocol acronym. Explain why this work belongs in a focused house.

Anchor: An AT-focused Subcult.tv initiative Extract delegated publication, consent-aware delivery and export/deletion contracts from the organizing prototype.

## Slide 2: The user and problem
Ask the listener whether this problem matches their actual experience. Do not invent interview quotes.

Anchor: Non-electoral member organizations and event coordinators Public communications and private consent/registration workflows are often mixed.

## Slide 3: What we propose
Describe one concrete workflow from beginning to end. Explain the scope deliberately excluded.

Anchor: Extract delegated publication, consent-aware delivery and export/deletion contracts from the organizing prototype. A bounded work package, not an all-at-once platform launch.

## Slide 4: What exists today
Offer the exact repository anchors from the proposal. No tests or live deployment were run for this funding review.

Anchor: Implemented integration paths; live foundation/PDS/provider operation not verified. Source evidence is not current production verification.

## Slide 5: The interoperability boundary
Walk through one allowed public field and one prohibited private field. Portability is not privacy or consent.

Anchor: Public explicitly approved event artifacts; private supporters, RSVPs, consent, delivery and analytics. Public records should be reusable; private operations stay deliberately separate.

## Slide 6: Delivery and proof
Explain who independently verifies acceptance and what happens if the test fails. Counts are proposed targets, not achieved metrics.

Anchor: Suppression immediately before delivery; revoked-delegation rejection; deletion/export fixture tests. Publish limitations and reproducible fixtures; retain failed evidence.

## Slide 7: Sustainability
Explain who would pay and why. All pricing or partnership assumptions still require validation.

Anchor: Paid setup and hosted private operations for non-electoral organizations. Compete with CRM complexity through a narrow reliable boundary, not an all-in-one campaign suite.

## Slide 8: The ask and decision
Make the bounded ask, then pause. Do not imply a specific funder has agreed or that funding automatically makes the product safe.

Anchor: Proposed $20,000 three-month privacy-boundary package, contingent on rights and charitable-scope review. No campaign execution in charitable scope, customer endorsement or guaranteed message delivery. Next: qualify scope, rights and counterpart before an agreement.

## Questions to prepare
Why not contribute to an existing project? What rights do you hold? Who will maintain this after the award? What evidence will an outsider reproduce? How much founder time is actually available? What fails closed if funding ends?
