# Membership Communications Speaker Notes
Companion to [[Membership Communications Deck]]. Target length: 5–7 minutes. State that this is a proposal and distinguish prototypes from qualified services.

## Slide 1: Membership Communications
Open with the beneficiary, not the protocol acronym. Explain why this work belongs in a focused house.

Anchor: An AT-focused Subcult.tv initiative Document and test entitlement/revocation across an AT-adjacent identity service and Matrix/OIDC.

## Slide 2: The user and problem
Ask the listener whether this problem matches their actual experience. Do not invent interview quotes.

Anchor: Organizations needing governed communication access Membership revocation and identity handoff can fail across independently operated communication systems.

## Slide 3: What we propose
Describe one concrete workflow from beginning to end. Explain the scope deliberately excluded.

Anchor: Document and test entitlement/revocation across an AT-adjacent identity service and Matrix/OIDC. A bounded work package, not an all-at-once platform launch.

## Slide 4: What exists today
Offer the exact repository anchors from the proposal. No tests or live deployment were run for this funding review.

Anchor: Local-contract architecture; direct AT publication not evidenced and live authorized account gates remain open. Source evidence is not current production verification.

## Slide 5: The interoperability boundary
Walk through one allowed public field and one prohibited private field. Portability is not privacy or consent.

Anchor: Private membership and communication metadata; no public membership graph. Public records should be reusable; private operations stay deliberately separate.

## Slide 6: Delivery and proof
Explain who independently verifies acceptance and what happens if the test fails. Counts are proposed targets, not achieved metrics.

Anchor: Two authorized test accounts in isolated topology; revocation and recovery evidence; independent review before security claims. Publish limitations and reproducible fixtures; retain failed evidence.

## Slide 7: Sustainability
Explain who would pay and why. All pricing or partnership assumptions still require validation.

Anchor: Infrastructure support contracts only after operational qualification. Use Matrix ecosystem components rather than claiming to replace them; no affiliation with Signal.

## Slide 8: The ask and decision
Make the bounded ask, then pause. Do not imply a specific funder has agreed or that funding automatically makes the product safe.

Anchor: Proposed $20,000 scoped interoperability and independent security review, not a new messenger launch. E2EE does not hide service metadata or defeat a compromised browser origin; no secure-production claim. Next: qualify scope, rights and counterpart before an agreement.

## Questions to prepare
Why not contribute to an existing project? What rights do you hold? Who will maintain this after the award? What evidence will an outsider reproduce? How much founder time is actually available? What fails closed if funding ends?
