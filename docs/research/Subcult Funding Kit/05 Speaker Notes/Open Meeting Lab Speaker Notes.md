# Open Meeting Lab Speaker Notes
Companion to [[Open Meeting Lab Deck]]. Target length: 5–7 minutes. State that this is a proposal and distinguish prototypes from qualified services.

## Slide 1: Open Meeting Lab
Open with the beneficiary, not the protocol acronym. Explain why this work belongs in a focused house.

Anchor: An AT-focused Subcult.tv initiative Develop the event-sourced simulator and reusable approved aggregate-publication contracts.

## Slide 2: The user and problem
Ask the listener whether this problem matches their actual experience. Do not invent interview quotes.

Anchor: Community facilitators and governance-software builders Meeting procedure and public outcomes need traceability without disclosing deliberation or ballots.

## Slide 3: What we propose
Describe one concrete workflow from beginning to end. Explain the scope deliberately excluded.

Anchor: Develop the event-sourced simulator and reusable approved aggregate-publication contracts. A bounded work package, not an all-at-once platform launch.

## Slide 4: What exists today
Offer the exact repository anchors from the proposal. No tests or live deployment were run for this funding review.

Anchor: Implemented meeting simulator; experimental AT adapter; explicitly no production PDS integration. Source evidence is not current production verification.

## Slide 5: The interoperability boundary
Walk through one allowed public field and one prohibited private field. Portability is not privacy or consent.

Anchor: Public approved aggregate outcomes; private attendance, rosters, deliberation, credentials and ballots. Public records should be reusable; private operations stay deliberately separate.

## Slide 6: Delivery and proof
Explain who independently verifies acceptance and what happens if the test fails. Counts are proposed targets, not achieved metrics.

Anchor: Replay deterministic fixtures; prove private fields cannot enter published artifacts; accessible facilitator rehearsal. Publish limitations and reproducible fixtures; retain failed evidence.

## Slide 7: Sustainability
Explain who would pay and why. All pricing or partnership assumptions still require validation.

Anchor: Training, support and grant-backed maintenance; rights reviewed for all instructional content. Complement facilitation tools with reproducibility and explicit publication control.

## Slide 8: The ask and decision
Make the bounded ask, then pause. Do not imply a specific funder has agreed or that funding automatically makes the product safe.

Anchor: Proposed $25,000 four-month public/private governance R&D package, not an election system. No secret ballots, binding voting, legal procedure certification or copied Robert's Rules text. Next: qualify scope, rights and counterpart before an agreement.

## Questions to prepare
Why not contribute to an existing project? What rights do you hold? Who will maintain this after the award? What evidence will an outsider reproduce? How much founder time is actually available? What fails closed if funding ends?
