# Runway Model
All figures are proposed USD cash budgets, not the founder's actual income, tax advice, quotes or committed funding. Gross compensation is not take-home pay. Confirm entity, taxes, health coverage, current cash and liabilities with an accountant.

## Lean founder baseline
| Cost | Monthly |
| --- | ---: |
| Founder gross compensation | 6,000 |
| Payroll/tax/benefit allowance | 1,000 |
| Hosting, backups and development services | 400 |
| Independent review / specialist accrual | 700 |
| Accounting, legal and administration | 400 |
| Community research, accessibility and travel allowance | 500 |
| Operating subtotal | 9,000 |
| Contingency reserve contribution | 1,000 |
| Total planning burn | 10,000 |

Six months: $60,000. Twelve months: $120,000. Eighteen months: $180,000.
The reserve is 10% of the total envelope (11.11% of the $9,000 operating subtotal). No grant is assumed to permit a generic contingency line; replace it with allowable costs or raise unrestricted reserve separately.

## Twelve-month allocation and capacity
Commons 45% = $54,000; culture 30% = $36,000; Patchwork qualification 15% = $18,000; fundraising/administration 10% = $12,000. Total $120,000 and 100%.
These are planning allocations, not statements that each funder can reimburse every cost. Actual timesheets and invoices govern restricted cost attribution. Individual project asks are modular alternatives; the $40,000 Patchwork option requires more funding or less other work than its $18,000 baseline allocation.

## Investor alternative: eighteen-month $250,000
Lean baseline $180,000 + additional contractor capacity $45,000 + commercialization and external review $15,000 + formation/fundraising/legal allowance $10,000 = $250,000.
No valuation, SAFE cap, discount, board rights or ownership percentage is proposed. Obtain counsel and founder decisions. An investor may reject a house model; narrow the investable entity to culture products if mission and ownership can remain coherent.

## Cash timing
Runway months = unrestricted usable cash / actual monthly cash burn.
Signed restricted awards enter a separate cash schedule with allowed-cost and payment dates. Receivables are not cash. Example: a $30,000 award paid entirely after a three-month milestone does not solve the first three months' $30,000 cash requirement.
Sponsor fee example, not a quote: to net $120,000 after a 10% fee on gross receipts, gross required = $120,000 / 0.90 = $133,333.34 rounded up to cents. Do not use $132,000. If an award cap is $120,000, 10% leaves $108,000, or 10.8 months at the planning burn.

Base forecast includes zero unawarded grants and zero hypothetical subscription revenue. Proposed 100 customers at $149/month is a market scenario only. Do not offset burn until paying customers and collections exist.

## Restricted award controls
Give each deliverable a unique cost code. Record actual hours, invoice, funding source, period, allowable share, payment status and evidence. No hour or invoice can be claimed twice. Disclose overlapping applications and adjust scope on award; Interledger's rules restrict concurrent active grants. See [[Funding Register]].
Review forecast monthly and trigger a scope reduction before cash drops below three months of committed obligations.
