# Portfolio and Boundaries
## Funding structure
| Lane | Purpose | Candidate source | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Commercial house | Culture product, hosted operations, paid implementation | Customers, aligned equity | No restricted grant subsidy of undisclosed private deliverables |
| Commons program | Reusable AT and open community infrastructure | Scoped grants, maintenance sponsorship | Open deliverables, independent governance, cost attribution |
| Protected public-benefit pilot | Patchwork safety and public-resource interoperability | Suitable sponsor/foundation | No service-readiness or impact claim before qualification |
| Incubator | Civic-suite experiments and adjacent media tools | Later, separately approved | Not booked as current revenue or grant outputs |
| Excluded | Empty dsa-pac and partisan campaign operations | None in this kit | No charitable budget or fundraising pitch |

A fiscal sponsor is not free money or a pass-through wallet. Negotiate control, permitted activities, fees, IP ownership, termination and transfer. Raft offers different sponsorship models; Open Source Collective is a 501(c)(6), not a charitable 501(c)(3). [Raft](https://raft.wiki/About-us), [OSC](https://docs.oscollective.org/welcome-and-introduction-to-osc/what-is-fiscal-hosting).

A US public-benefit corporation is not thereby a tax-exempt charity. Do not form multiple entities solely to pursue this draft without legal/accounting advice. A sensible first step is a fit discussion about a bounded commons project while keeping commercial ownership clear.

## Political and organizational guardrails
Section 501(c)(3) organizations cannot intervene in candidate campaigns. This is not a blanket ban on every civic or policy activity, and the proper classification requires counsel. This kit excludes candidate support/opposition, campaign coordination, voter profiling and partisan messaging from charitable proposals. [IRS restriction](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/restriction-of-political-campaign-intervention-by-section-501c3-tax-exempt-organizations).

DSA repository names establish neither affiliation nor endorsement. No official marks, member access, chapter users or organization authority may be claimed without written authorization. Generalizing code must change actual scope, governance and data handling; changing the label is insufficient.

## Rights and portfolio scope
Patchwork is GPL-3.0-or-later; dsa-proto, action-network, dsa-signal and dsa-slate are GPL-3.0-only; roberts-rules is AGPL-3.0-only. No project-wide license was found in dsa-forum or dsa-seats. Do not promise permissive relicensing of code you do not exclusively own. Audit dependencies and contributor rights before extraction. See [[DSA Evidence]] and [[Patchwork Evidence]].

Subcult-tv is the brand layer and subcult-pds an infrastructure candidate, not an audited hosting business. CLPR, HasanAra, transcript-create, Soundhash, Assmold and Cutroom may inform a later media/evidence track. Their README-level inventory is not proof of AT integration, current ownership, active staffing or grant eligibility. Keep them off the primary funding ask until a separate diligence pass.

## Public versus private
Public: deliberately published artist/event records, approved public resource information, narrowly approved organizational artifacts, non-sensitive provenance.
Private: contact lists, consent receipts, RSVPs, member affiliation, precise personal location, moderation evidence, financial records, ballots, attendance, communications and credentials.
Deletion from an authoritative repository cannot guarantee erasure of all downstream copies. Do not advertise global deletion, perfect privacy or a universal private-data AT layer.
