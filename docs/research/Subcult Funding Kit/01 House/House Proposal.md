# House Proposal
## Proposition
Subcult.tv is proposed as a US-based, AT Protocol-focused software house building tools through which independent cultural communities and community organizations can maintain durable public identities and records without surrendering their private operations.

The house has one thesis, not a requirement to launch every repository: **portable public coordination, locally controlled private work, and open infrastructure others can reuse**. The first commercial product combines Subcults' scene, artist, venue and event records with Subcult OS's organizer workflows. Patchwork supplies a demanding public-resource and mutual-aid research setting. Selected civic-suite components contribute organizational authority, publication boundaries, governance and provenance patterns.

## Why this house
A login integration alone does not make an interoperable application. Subcult's opportunity is the difficult operational layer: how records are authored, corrected, indexed, migrated and removed; how organizations authorize publication; and how public discovery stays separate from sensitive coordination. AT supplies public repository and identity primitives; application semantics and operational guarantees still need implementation. [Protocol overview](https://atproto.com/specs/atp).

## First customer and wedge
Start with independent venues, promoters and artist collectives managing recurring events in one regional scene. The initial buyer is an operator coordinating multiple events, not every fan. Offer a coherent public event/artist presence and private staffing, commitments and communications workflow. Validate willingness to pay through paid, bounded implementation pilots before broad consumer acquisition.

Proposed product packaging: free public discovery/identity; paid organizational workspace, hosted operations and support; optional migration/integration services. Test $49, $149 and $399 monthly organization tiers as hypotheses, not current prices. Keep audience contact exports governed by consent rather than treating a public follow as marketing permission.

## Public-benefit program
Subcult Commons is a proposed separately accounted program delivering independent-reader fixtures, projection recovery, safe publication contracts, migration guides and organizational authority experiments. Patchwork and governance prototypes act as reference consumers. Success is external reuse and independently repeatable evidence, not simply adding features to Subcult's proprietary advantage.

## Operating model
One founder leads engineering, product and stewardship. Buy bounded independent privacy/security, accessibility and accounting reviews; do not represent unnamed contractors as employees. Limit active delivery to the cultural pilot and one commons work package. All other repos remain incubating, evidence sources, or explicitly excluded.

Proposed first-year time allocation: Commons 45%, culture pilot 30%, Patchwork qualification 15%, fundraising/administration 10%. Every paid hour is allocated once. Civic-suite work enters through the commons extraction allocation; it is not eight additional simultaneous product commitments.

## Money and milestones
The base operating scenario is $120,000 for twelve months, including a $6,000/month gross founder compensation assumption and a reserve. It is a planning envelope, not a promise that a single grant will cover the house. The separate investor alternative is $250,000 over eighteen months, with added contractor capacity and commercialization costs. They are alternatives or transparently reconciled cofunding, never additive claims over the same work. See [[Runway Model]].

By month 3: rights inventory, two independently operated fixture consumers as a target, and three design-partner conversations. By month 6: a paid culture pilot target and reproducible record lifecycle/rebuild evidence. By month 12: a second independent implementation target, renewal evidence from an operator cohort, and public maintenance/governance documentation. All counts are future acceptance targets, not traction.

## Sustainability
Customer revenue can pay for hosting, implementation and continued product development. Restricted grants support defined commons outputs. Sponsorship supports maintenance where actual dependencies justify it. Avoid financing recurring obligations with unawarded grants. If funding or partner access fails, reduce to one maintained open component plus paid integration work.

## Risks and decisions
The largest risks are founder concentration, unvalidated buyer demand, operational/privacy obligations, fragmented schemas, licensing gaps and funder fit. Mitigate through scope caps, independent reviews, explicit data boundaries and staged deployment gates. An AT house should be known for dependable contributions, not a large logo wall.
